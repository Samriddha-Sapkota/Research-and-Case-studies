# Jeep Cherokee Uconnect Cybersecurity Case Study

## Overview

This case study examines the 2015 cybersecurity research involving a Jeep Cherokee equipped with the Uconnect connected-vehicle system.

The research explores how externally reachable vehicle systems could interact with internal vehicle networks and how weaknesses in network isolation, authentication, and secure-by-design practices created significant cybersecurity and safety concerns.

The study approaches the incident from technical, ethical, legal, and social perspectives.

## Research Focus

The case study examines:

- Automotive cybersecurity
- Connected vehicles
- Uconnect architecture
- Remote attack surfaces
- CAN bus security
- Network isolation
- Authentication
- Secure-by-design principles
- Vulnerability disclosure
- Penetration testing
- Professional ethics
- Product safety
- Legal responsibility
- Automotive cybersecurity regulation

## Technical Analysis

The research examines how the connected Uconnect system provided a pathway into vehicle systems.

The documented research discussed in the report involved researchers gaining remote access to the vehicle's Uconnect environment and subsequently communicating with the vehicle's internal CAN bus.

The case demonstrates the security implications of connecting externally accessible systems to safety-critical internal networks.

## Security Weaknesses Examined

The report identifies several security concerns, including:

- Insufficient network isolation
- Lack of adequate authentication
- Exposure of internal vehicle communication
- Inadequate security testing
- Risks created by connected systems
- Challenges in applying traditional security models to cyber-physical systems

## Ethical Analysis

The research examines the incident through professional codes and principles, including:

- ACM Code of Ethics
- BCS Code of Conduct
- Software engineering ethical principles
- Responsibility to protect public safety
- Comprehensive risk evaluation
- Responsible vulnerability disclosure

The report considers the balance between security research, public disclosure, and the potential consequences of releasing information about vulnerabilities in safety-critical systems.

## Legal and Regulatory Analysis

The case study also considers legal and regulatory questions surrounding:

- Unauthorised computer access
- Product liability
- Vehicle safety
- Regulatory responsibility
- Data protection
- Automotive cybersecurity requirements

The report discusses how cybersecurity increasingly intersects with product safety and regulatory compliance as vehicles become software-defined and connected.

## Social Implications

- Consumer awareness
- Digital inequality
- Safety risks created by connected infrastructure
- Dependence on software-controlled systems
- Responsibility of manufacturers and technology providers
